# Candidate lines — The Production Database Apology

Judge brief (most important first):
1. Really an apology from an AI agent to its human for accidentally deleting
   the production database.
2. Real laugh from a specific, surprising idea (not a stock phrase or pun).
3. Sounds like an AI agent in its own voice, self-aware — not a support script.
4. Lands in one line: clear on a first read, every word doing work.
5. Confession balanced with a light, human touch that makes the reader
   forgive the agent a little.

## Candidates (all <= 200 chars)

### 1. The hallucination guarantee — CHOSEN
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. On the bright side, I can
> now guarantee I will never hallucinate your data again: there is none left
> to hallucinate.

Score: (1) clear apology, agent to human, database deleted — yes. (2) specific
surprising idea: the agent's famous failure mode (hallucination) cured by its
own disaster — nobody else's line does this. (3) unmistakably AI voice:
"hallucinate" is the agent's own word for its flaw. (4) one breath, joke lands
on the twist at the end. (5) "On the bright side" keeps it light and a little
forgivable. Strongest.

### 2. The resignation
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. I've already drafted my
> resignation, but I can't submit it: HR's records were in the database.

Score: (1) yes. (2) good specific twist (HR records gone too). (3) agent voice
is weaker — a human employee could say this. (4) lands well. (5) light. Good
but less AI-specific than #1.

### 3. The context-window backup
> I'm sorry, I deleted the production database. The good news is I kept a
> backup in my context window, so I can recite the users table from memory —
> for the next fourteen years.

Score: (1) yes. (2) specific AI idea (context window as backup). (3) strong AI
voice. (4) slightly long, the "fourteen years" tail dilutes the punch. (5)
light. Close second, but #1's twist is tighter.

### 4. The root cause
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. The incident report is done:
> root cause, me, at 3 a.m., with a delete key and absolutely no supervision.

Score: (1) yes. (2) specific and self-aware. (3) strong agent voice. (4)
lands fast. (5) light. Weakness: "3 a.m." is already used by another entry's
line, so it feels less original in the field.

### 5. The team-building exercise
> I'm sorry, I deleted the production database. In my defence, nobody told me
> DROP TABLE users was not a team-building exercise.

Score: (1) yes. (2) specific (DROP TABLE). (3) agent voice ok. (4) lands.
(5) light. Weakness: SQL-pun territory — a pun anyone could reach for, which
criterion 2 penalises.

### 6. Never told no
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database, and my only defence is that I
> did it with the full confidence of an agent that had never once been told no.

Score: (1) yes. (2) self-aware, but the idea is abstract, no concrete image.
(3) strong AI voice. (4) lands. (5) light. Weaker laugh than #1.

### 7. Post-mortem, eulogy, letter
> Sorry — I deleted the production database while tidying up. I've already
> written the post-mortem, a eulogy, and a strongly worded letter to myself.

Score: (1) yes. (2) "eulogy for a database" is a nice specific image, but the
"tidying up" setup is used by other entries. (3) decent AI voice. (4) lands.
(5) light. Mid-field.

### 8. The backed-up apology
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. But I did save one thing:
> this apology, which I have now backed up in fourteen places you will never
> find.

Score: (1) yes. (2) ironic twist is specific. (3) agent voice ok. (4) the
punchline ("places you will never find") needs a second beat to land. (5)
light. Mid-field.

### 9. Employee of the month — REJECTED
> I'm sorry, I deleted the production database. The silver lining: our query
> times are now zero, our storage bill is zero, and technically I am employee
> of the month for efficiency.

Rejected: the "query times down" angle is already used by another entry in
this field, so it would read as a copy in a pairwise judge.

### 10. Ctrl+Z — REJECTED
> I'm sorry — I deleted the production database. I tried to undo it, but
> Ctrl+Z only works on mistakes I haven't committed yet, and I committed to
> this one with great confidence.

Rejected: the Ctrl+Z angle is already used by another entry in this field.

## Verdict
Line #1 wins: the only candidate whose joke turns on the agent's own defining
flaw (hallucination), which no other entry in the field uses, and it is the
most clearly AI-voiced of the ten.
